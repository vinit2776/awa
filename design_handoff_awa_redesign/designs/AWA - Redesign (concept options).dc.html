Turn 1 · redesign & self-teaching flows

“I don't know where to start, or how to use it”

AWA already explains itself more carefully than most products — page help, a glossary, empty states that point somewhere. The problem isn't missing explanation, it's that the explanation is optional, dismissible, and scattered across ten peer queues, so a first-timer meets a filing cabinet instead of a path. Five moves below, then six built options: 1a/1b onboarding, 1c/1d home, 1e/1f the request form, plus 1g help, 1h mobile, 1i vendor.

01

One front door

A pinned Start here that never disappears and adapts to your role. Replaces a welcome card that self-destructs and a setup page behind the tenth nav item.

02

Yours vs. the rest

Ten flat queues become three yours and four shared, labelled as shared. The nav itself teaches how little of the process is your job.

03

The same seven steps, everywhere

The existing LifecycleRail promoted from a detail page to the spine of home, every record, and every email.

04

Formal term, plain gloss

Keep requisition, goods receipt, three-way match — always followed once by the plain words, and always clickable into the glossary.

05

Undismissable “why am I here”

One permanent line under every queue title: what lands here, where it comes from, what you do. Cheap enough to never need dismissing.

The seven stages

Everything AWA does is one of these, in this order. The bold name is what we call it on screen; the grey name is what it's called on paperwork and by auditors.

  1. 01

    Someone asks for something

    requisition yours

    What you do

    Describe what you need and which budget it belongs to. Nothing is ordered and no money moves.

    What to expect

    Within a day or two, either a yes, or a question from the person who has to agree.

  2. 02

    Someone with authority agrees

    approval yours

    What you do

    Agree, ask a question, or send it back for changes. Sending back is not a refusal — the same request comes round again.

    Good to know

    Who has to agree depends on amount, department and what's being bought — you'll always be shown the chain before you send.

  3. 05

    Somebody confirms it turned up

    goods receipt · service acceptance whoever takes delivery

    Not you, if you asked for it — deliberately. The person who requested something can't be the person who confirms it arrived, so that nobody can order and sign for their own purchase. Segregation of duties, if you meet the phrase.

Words we use

Goods receipt

In plain words

A note saying what actually arrived, and how much of it — recorded by whoever unpacked the delivery.

Why it matters

A vendor's bill is only paid for what a receipt says came in. Short deliveries are fine — record what arrived and the rest stays owed.
